How season rating is earned

Rating comes from two places: holding territories and capturing them. Holding pays out every 5 seconds, on an hourly rate that depends on how many territories the guild owns:

yield = Σi=0..t base × s × r[min(i, len(r) − 1)] base = 120/h, capture = 40 × s

r is a regression table that punishes hoarding: territory 1 counts triple, territory 5 counts 80%, and everything from the 22nd onward counts only 20%. So the 99th territory is worth a fifteenth of the first. s is a global scalar that Wynncraft raises by hand at points during the season — typically stepping 1.5 → 2.0 → 3.0 → 5.0 → 10.0.

Guild raids pay rating as well, built up per player: 50 base, plus 10 / 20 / 30 for that player's first, second and third gambit. One player is worth 50–110, so a full party of four tops out at 440 per raid. Measured against the poll data the average lands near 240, which is a four-player party running few gambits.

The decisive detail: raid rating does not move with the scalar. Calibrating holding on raid-free blocks and dividing the leftover by raids completed gives 251 SR/raid while s was 3.0 and 230 once s was 5.0 — a ratio of 0.92, flat, where scaling would have shown 1.67. So a bump doubles holding and capturing but leaves the raid share alone, and the effective multiplier on the total rate is about 1.88, not 2.00. The projection splits the rate accordingly.

The scalar can be read straight out of the poll data. Over intervals with no war and no territory change, holding is the only source, so ΔSR ÷ (base × Σr × hours) is the scalar. Aggregated into 30-minute blocks with no raid in them, it reads s = 2.89 for 10–18 Sep and s = 5.00 from 19 Sep on, with an implied base of 120.0 — exactly the wiki figure. So the season stepped 3.0 → 5.0 on 19 Sep, and the current scalar is 5.0.
